## Audit scope and authority

This audit evaluates the persisted `prototype/argon-e01/` state in a browser and against the production Design System at `/design-system`, the Runtime Architecture, and ADR-0001. CDI canonical contracts outrank ARGON. ARGON is experimental evidence; this document does not promote its implementation, visual approval, clinic copy, assets, or behavior to production authority.

Read authorities included `DESIGN.md`, `docs/README.md`, Product Constitution, Agent Contract, Decision Recovery Ledger, V1 Scope, Open Decisions, Runtime Architecture, ADR-0001, and the applicable Design System authorities: Validation Harness, Visual Worlds, Presentation Contexts, Composition Contracts, Design QA, Responsive Layout, Tokens, Typography, Photography, Motion, Components, Signature Patterns, Controlled Variation, Experience Direction, and bidirectional accessibility.

The executable comparison surface is `src/design-system/**` and `/design-system`, including its registered Visual Worlds, semantic role map, shared action/form/media/layout primitives, composition fixtures, and reduced-motion behavior. The implementation findings below concern differences from those actual shared contracts, not merely stylistic similarity to the prose specifications.

## Rendered contexts and methods

ARGON was served from its documented static-server mechanism at `http://127.0.0.1:4178/`; the existing server was confirmed to serve the current `prototype/argon-e01/` directory. Chromium captured the persisted files, with WebGL active, at:

- `390×844`, `430×932`, `1024×768`, and `1440×900` in Persian (`fa-IR`, RTL).
- `390×844` and `1440×900` in English (`en`, LTR).
- The full journey at `390×844` Persian; desktop captures at `1440×900` for the principal discovery, Laser event, treatment information, doctor, technology, and consultation states.
- A live `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` capture was taken at the Laser event midpoint; the browser reports the reduced preference, a static focus field, and a `0.00001s` scene transition. No source changes were made for this audit.

All 12 instrumented direct-state captures rendered without browser console/page errors or horizontal overflow; the additional desktop states were visually reviewed. An interactive browser walk reached Treatments → Laser event → Laser information → Doctor → Technology → Consultation. Scene changes left `location.href` unchanged; locale changes updated document text/`lang`/`dir` without changing the URL; the ARGON wordmark reset the state and used `replaceState`. On a `390×500` keyboard-height emulation, the consultation submit button and status were below the viewport and the fixed page could not scroll. This is a browser geometry stress check, not physical-device evidence.

Rendered files and machine-readable capture, navigation, performance and accessibility observations are indexed in [`evidence/argon-design-system-audit-01/README.md`](../../evidence/argon-design-system-audit-01/README.md). Existing prototype evidence was also reviewed but not treated as a substitute for these current captures.

## Executive finding

ARGON has a strong **Dark Cinematic** visual direction and a usable sequence of scene concepts: human-centered arrival, treatment discovery, restrained sensory treatment identity, readable facts, clinician trust, clinic/device context, and a consultation action. The dark photographic world is coherent from hero through consultation. The mobile scenes demonstrate several genuine recompositions rather than just shrinking desktop layouts.

The migration target is the **experience concepts and selected composition relationships**, rebuilt on the production Design System and route-resolved semantic content. ARGON's fixed, non-scroll, JavaScript-owned state shell, global input interception, duplicated hard-coded treatment content, bespoke token/CSS system, WebGL renderer, and demonstration media are not production foundations. The fixed state model fails URL/history/deep-link behavior after entry, excludes most content from the no-JavaScript base, can leave focus inside a newly hidden scene, and clips the consultation CTA when the virtual keyboard reduces usable height. V1 routes and semantic document content must remain accessible without cinematic behavior.

**Overall direction:** preserve the restrained cinematic journey idea and specific scene concepts; refactor each composition into the canonical rails/zones and shared primitives; replace the prototype navigation/data/rendering implementation and all demo content/assets. Retire dormant Renewal shader behavior and unused demo media. No current evidence justifies a Design System gap or a fifth Visual World.

## Visual World fit

**Closest approved world: DARK CINEMATIC.** Evidence: near-black/charcoal fields, warm neutral highlights, high-contrast editorial type, immersive face-led image treatment, restrained overall chrome, and consistent dark tonal treatment. This is a directional classification, not approval of the ARGON palette. ARGON's literal colors (`#242321`, translucent whites, and hard-coded overlays) are a separate prototype palette; production must use the Dark Cinematic registry mapping in semantic roles. No ARGON-specific world or theme is warranted. The warm photograph is not authority to make gold the control color.

## Navigation and state decision

**Prototype state-navigation architecture: REPLACE.** The direct state query is useful as a presentation/debug fixture, but it is not a production URL contract. After interactions, the URL remains `/`; browser Back cannot return to the prior scene; reload returns to the hero unless a state query was entered; state, locale, and history are not synchronized. The fixed world sets `html, body { overflow: hidden }`, fixes the root stage to the viewport, globally prevents wheel scrolling, and intercepts touch swipes and arrow keys. Inactive sections are hidden from the accessibility tree. This makes scene motion and JS a prerequisite for content progression and weakens SEO/deep linking.

CDI's full-stage grammar already permits a scene to occupy a stage or scroll region and defines scenes as semantic units; it explicitly makes routes/URLs independent from visual transitions and says transitions never carry content access. ADR-0001 requires server-rendered semantic content with scoped client enhancement. Therefore the production baseline should be normal route/document semantics and native history; cinematic visual transitions can enhance route/section changes without hijacking scroll or owning state. This is a direct implementation correction, not a request for a new system rule.

## Continuity and cinematic-to-editorial handoff

**Continuous World: PARTIAL.** The ARGON Dark Cinematic expression persists across Hero, Discovery, Laser, information, Doctor, Technology and Consultation; the Technology scene moves to a clinic/device image while retaining dark overlays and the same masthead/type. There is no abrupt generic white-site reset. The limitation is that most scenes retain the same portrait as the full-bleed background, so the world does not always evolve with each content role and the total journey is a set of view-sized overlays rather than a continuous scroll narrative.

**Cinematic → Editorial handoff: PARTIAL.** The Laser experience resolves into text/facts, then provider/equipment context and an action; motion becomes less prominent on these screens. Yet the information, trust and form scenes still occupy a full-screen cinematic shell over imagery. Production should let Treatment Information, Doctor facts and consultation remain semantic/editorial content-first regions; the same Visual World can continue through typography, tones and media without a persistent cinematic client layer.

## Negative space

- **Purposeful:** the broad dark field opposite the face in desktop Hero supports its text-safe region; the Doctor copy/media separation protects the portrait and establishes hierarchy; open space around the discovery list and information facts maintains a calm editorial pace.
- **Needs composition review:** the mobile Consultation content ends well above the viewport bottom, leaving a large unassigned lower field while the form submit is in normal content flow. Do not “fill” it with decoration; recompose around keyboard-safe usable height and Action Zone placement. At shorter keyboard-height emulation, the submit is actually clipped, so the void at normal height is not a substitute for a responsive action contract.

## Performance and execution

On a cold browser page load from the local static server, current performance entries transferred approximately **957 KB**: the font (~111 KB), hero portrait (~41 KB), doctor image (~302 KB), technology image (~376 KB), renewal material (~65 KB), CSS (~29 KB), and two JS files (~33 KB). The doctor and technology images are preloaded despite belonging to later scenes. `material.js` creates a viewport-sized WebGL canvas, uploads portrait and renewal textures, caps DPR, and schedules draws on progress/pointer events; `experience.js` uses requestAnimationFrame during transitions. There is no always-running RAF loop, which is positive. This is a local transfer observation, not a production network benchmark.

For production, keep high-priority hero media separate from below-the-fold doctor/device media; defer later imagery; use responsive/art-directed source selection and reserved media geometry. Do not port the WebGL renderer or full-viewport canvas as a default runtime. If a justified cinematic scene later needs expensive client execution, scope it to that scene with lifecycle cleanup and reduced-motion fallback. Editorial information and forms must not inherit its JS/canvas/media payload. Ordinary routes, semantic server output and publication-aware caching belong to the Next.js architecture; no caching behavior in ARGON is reusable.

## Experience narrative finding

**PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED.** The experience supports `ACT I — FEEL` (Hero/Laser), `ACT II — UNDERSTAND` (treatment facts), `ACT III — TRUST` (doctor and setting), and `ACT V — ACT` (consultation). It has no governed evidence scene, so Act IV is absent. Retain this five-part sequence as a useful planning lens only; the Product Constitution's concern/known-treatment journeys and capability-derived content remain the binding flow. Do not force five acts onto every clinic or treatment.

## System gaps and Owner decisions

- **System gap candidates:** NONE. The desired transitions, full-stage layouts, motion intent, media composition, reduced motion, navigation, and routes are already covered by canonical contracts. ARGON's unsupported state model is an implementation experiment that must conform, not a reusable platform gap.
- **Owner decisions required:** NONE for this audit or for starting a canonical production implementation. Eventual clinic-specific media and Visual World/controlled-variation acceptance remain normal product/content review boundaries; this audit does not claim Owner approval of ARGON or its screenshots.
- **SAFE-01:** remains `NOT_YET_TESTABLE`. These are browser viewports/emulation only; no physical iPhone/Android notch, gesture area, dynamic browser chrome, or actual virtual keyboard was used. The explicit short-height emulation exposed a clipped form action, but does not close physical-device QA.

## Risks and exit decision

1. **State-shell carryover risk:** porting the no-scroll demo model would lose routes, history, indexable information and accessible keyboard/focus behavior. Guardrail: build semantic routes first; enhancement cannot own access.
2. **Demo-content risk:** fake clinician/device/contact details look plausible in the cinematic shell. Guardrail: do not migrate demonstration data/assets; enforce clinic content/publication sources.
3. **Efficacy-impression risk:** a scan crossing a person's face could be misconstrued as an actual treatment procedure despite no result change. Guardrail: use an abstract sensory treatment identity with explanatory semantics, no result morph, and separate governed evidence.
4. **Payload risk:** preloading later images and initializing a full-viewport WebGL canvas undermine editorial-runtime isolation. Guardrail: prioritized media and scoped optional enhancement.
5. **Viewport/safe-area risk:** viewport-fixed layout clips the consultation CTA under keyboard-height changes. Guardrail: route/document flow, Action Zone and physical device validation before release.

**Exit decision: `ARGON_MIGRATION_READY`.** The production migration map identifies concepts to carry and prototype architecture/content to replace. This means a production experience milestone can begin from canonical CDI contracts; it does not approve ARGON as a production design or make its files reusable.

## Next milestone

Build a **canonical Clinic Experience vertical slice** on the existing Next.js foundation: one semantic, indexable route using clearly identified representative structured content fixtures, a shared Dark Cinematic composition, capability-filtered treatment discovery, and an optional reduced-motion-safe Laser signature island. Establish real route/history/focus behavior and the Consultation Action Zone before expanding scene coverage. Keep clinic identity/content as fixtures until provisioning and real production content/assets are approved; do not migrate ARGON code/assets.
