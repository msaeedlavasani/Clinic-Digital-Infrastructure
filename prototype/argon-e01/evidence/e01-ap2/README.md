# E01-AP2 — Subsurface Light as a World Event

Three generated 16:9 art-production studies advancing the AP1-A direction. They are conceptual images, not Argon device photography, medical visualization, or a claim about laser physics. All optical and subsurface behavior is experiential metaphor. No runtime code or motion was created.

Gate ratings describe the visible stills, not the quality of the generation process. No study clears all required advancement gates in this review; this is evidence for Owner and Design Review, not a direction selection.

## A2-1 — Intimate / Macro

**CONCEPT**  Skin fills the frame; the treatment is a small, precise interruption of an otherwise human, tactile surface.

**WORLD_EVENT**  Texture and focus appear to tighten around the contact point, but the response stays almost entirely on the cheek.

**SKIN**  Natural texture is visible, though the extreme pore and fine-hair detail edges toward clinical inspection.

**DEPTH**  Shallow focus and a localized highlight imply depth. The image does not clearly show light moving within the skin.

**TECHNOLOGY**  A small optical treatment tip at frame edge points directly at the focal region.

**LUXURY**  Intimate crop, controlled light, and restrained color; no decorative interface or product display.

**MOTION_SEQUENCE**  BEFORE: still macro surface. EVENT: optical focus resolves at a tiny point. RESPONSE: local texture/focus subtly shifts. SETTLE: the field fades into natural skin. Not implemented.

**RISK**  The bright spot can read as cosmetic highlight or skin examination; high texture may overpower the premium treatment intent.

## A2-2 — Spatial / Cinematic

**CONCEPT**  A quiet treatment scene uses skin, a small optical tip, glass, and background depth as one connected photographic space.

**WORLD_EVENT**  A point of light appears on the cheek; a glass reflection and background focus seem to bend near the same region, though the environmental response is subtle.

**SKIN**  Believable profile with natural texture and calm expression.

**DEPTH**  Foreground treatment tip, face, glass plane, and soft architecture create layered perspective.

**TECHNOLOGY**  A cropped tip is directed toward the cheek and remains a small contextual object.

**LUXURY**  Negative space, soft mineral light, and restrained treatment context create a cinematic frame.

**MOTION_SEQUENCE**  BEFORE: reflection lines and face sit still. EVENT: cheek focus resolves. RESPONSE: nearby glass/reflection and depth shift subtly with it. SETTLE: space returns to stillness around the treated region. Not implemented.

**RISK**  It can still read as premium treatment photography with a bright cheek point; the spatial refraction may be missed at normal viewing size.

## A2-3 — Transition Frame

**CONCEPT**  A still caught while one local region of the portrait is changing optical state, with natural and altered material coexisting.

**WORLD_EVENT**  A small cheek region shows changed focus and surface response while the surrounding face remains photographic; the effect does not convincingly extend into the wider scene.

**SKIN**  Human anatomy remains plausible, but the altered cheek texture looks smudged and less photographic than the untreated area.

**DEPTH**  Close profile and background separation provide depth; the transition itself does not add convincing subsurface volume.

**TECHNOLOGY**  A partial aperture at the edge is aimed at the altered area, but the causal relationship is faint.

**LUXURY**  Controlled crop and neutral palette; the effect risks undermining the otherwise premium portrait.

**MOTION_SEQUENCE**  BEFORE: natural photographic face. EVENT: tiny optical focus resolves. RESPONSE: a soft asymmetric area changes local texture and focus. SETTLE: the altered area becomes a stable treatment state. Not implemented.

**RISK**  The cheek patch can read as a beauty filter, retouching artifact, or generic VFX rather than a treatment-world transition.
